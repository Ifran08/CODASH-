Choosing between a template and a custom build

AUG 2026

One of the first questions we get on nearly every discovery call is whether a template or page builder could get the job done. It's a fair question, and honestly, sometimes the answer is yes. A template is a real tool with a real use case. The trick is knowing when it fits and when it quietly turns into a liability.

When a template genuinely works

If you need a simple presence online fast, a landing page to validate an idea, or a placeholder while you figure out your actual business model, a template can carry you a long way. It's cheap, it's quick, and for that stage, quick is often the right priority.

Where templates start to cost more than they saved

The trouble shows up once your business grows past what the template was built for. Suddenly you need a custom booking flow, or a checkout that matches your exact process, or a page structure the builder simply doesn't support. At that point, teams often find themselves fighting the platform instead of building on it.

  • Every business ends up looking similar because everyone is pulling from the same block library.
  • Performance suffers, since templates load far more code than any single site actually uses.
  • Integrations with your own tools become limited or awkward.
  • Migrating off the platform later means starting over, not upgrading.

How we think about it

We don't push custom builds on everyone by default. Instead we ask what the site actually needs to do in the next year, not just today. If the answer involves specific workflows, integrations, or a brand experience that needs to stand apart, a custom build pays for itself quickly. If the need is genuinely simple and short term, we'll say so.

The right question isn't "template or custom." It's "what does this business need to do that a template can't."

A custom build gives you a site that's shaped entirely around your business rather than the other way around, and it gives you room to grow without hitting a wall the platform put there. That's usually where the real savings show up, not on day one, but a year or two down the line when your competitor is still stuck rebuilding.
