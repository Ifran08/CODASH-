Next.js vs a plain React build, how we decide

MAY 2026

Tech stack decisions get treated like a matter of taste sometimes, but for us they come down to what a specific project actually needs. Next.js and a plain React build can both produce a great result, they're just suited to different situations.

When we reach for Next.js

Next.js earns its place when a site needs strong search visibility, multiple content heavy pages, or server rendered data that changes often. Its built in routing and rendering options make it a strong fit for marketing sites, blogs, and anything where search engines finding your content matters as much as the visitor experience.

When plain React makes more sense

For a highly interactive application, a dashboard, or a tool that lives mostly behind a login where search visibility isn't a factor, a plain React build can be lighter and faster to develop. There's less framework overhead to account for, and the app can be structured exactly around the interactions it needs to support.

The checklist we actually use

  • Does this project need to rank well in search results?
  • Is most of the content public, or mostly behind a login?
  • How much of the experience depends on real time interaction versus displaying information?
  • What does the team maintaining this site after launch need to be comfortable with?

None of these questions have a universally right answer. They're specific to what a business actually needs the site to do, which is exactly why we walk through them on every project rather than defaulting to one stack out of habit.

The best stack is the one that disappears, because it fits the project so well nobody ever has to think about it again.

Whichever direction a project goes, the deciding factor is always the same: what will genuinely serve the business best over the next few years, not which technology happens to be trending this month.
