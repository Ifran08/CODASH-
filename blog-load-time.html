Why your site's load time is a design decision

JUL 2026

Load time usually gets treated as a developer's problem to solve after the design is already locked in. By the time anyone measures it, the layout has already committed to a hero video, a dozen custom fonts, and a carousel stacked with high resolution images. At that point, speed becomes a cleanup job instead of a design choice.

Every visual choice has a cost

A large hero image, an animated background, an embedded video, each one adds weight the browser has to download and render before a visitor sees anything useful. None of these choices are wrong on their own. The problem is when nobody weighs the visual payoff against the actual cost in loading time.

What we do differently

We think about performance while the design is still taking shape, not after. That means choosing image formats that compress well without losing quality, limiting font weights to what's actually used, and being deliberate about animation instead of adding it everywhere it might look nice.

  • Images are sized and compressed for exactly where they appear, not uploaded at full resolution.
  • Fonts are limited to the weights the design actually calls for.
  • Animation and motion are used to guide attention, not as decoration on every element.
  • Code is loaded in the order the page actually needs it, so the first thing a visitor sees appears fast.

Why it matters beyond the numbers

A fast site isn't just a technical win. Visitors form an impression of your business in the first couple of seconds, and a page that loads instantly signals competence before they've read a single word. A page that stalls does the opposite, no matter how good the design looks once it finally appears.

This is part of why we build design and development together rather than as separate stages. Speed has to be part of the conversation from the first mockup, because by the time a slow site reaches a developer, most of the damage is already baked into the design.
